How the test works

  1. 1Hold the phone at reading distanceThe front camera measures eye-to-screen distance from the size of your iris. Images never leave the phone.
  2. 2Cover one eye, answer the tiltEach faint patch leans left, stands upright or leans right. Tap the matching button. 45 patches per eye.
  3. 3Read your result, keep the recordOne headline says what to do next. Every patch shown and every answer given is kept with the conditions.

What contrast sensitivity measures

How faint a pattern each eye can still see, at five bar widths from coarse (1.5 cycles per degree) to fine (18). It depends on the clarity of the eye and on processing in the retina, optic nerve and brain, and often changes before a standard letter chart does.

Note 1. Cycles per degree counts light–dark bar pairs within one degree of visual angle, so the phone must be at a known distance; the camera measures it.

Note 2. Each column has nine contrast levels, from 28.6 % down to 0.5 %. The three faintest are exploratory: whether a phone screen can show them as intended has not yet been measured.

Why track it

Visual function and ageing. Contrast sensitivity declines with age, cataract and other eye conditions, and is linked to falls and night-time driving.

Environmental health and the exposome. Group studies have reported lower contrast sensitivity in occupants of water-damaged buildings and after some neurotoxic exposures. These are group findings; they do not allow any one person's result to be traced to an exposure.

Your own baseline. Two runs set it; repeat every few months under the same conditions and see whether anything changes.

Limits. MouldVision cannot detect mould or any exposure, and cannot diagnose any condition. A low result has many ordinary causes. Whether a building has a mould problem is answered by sampling the building itself.
